The Twist: Answer Key

Reveal this only at the end of the session, after each team has debated their case.

🧠

Youssef

Team 1 · "The Smart One"

🔒 Fixed mindset

Believes ability is a fixed trait he already has ("the smart one"). Avoids anything that could disprove that: he doesn't retake the test, dismisses the course, deletes his own question so he doesn't look unsure, and calls a low score "easy" to protect his image instead of engaging with the challenge.

📓

Mariam

Team 2 · "The Quiet Worker"

🎭 Imposter syndrome

Her results are genuinely strong — early finish, big improvement, helping classmates — but she can't accept the evidence. She explains success away ("only because I retook it"), rates herself far below reality, and privately considers quitting despite outperforming.

🏀

Nour

Team 3 · "The Planner in Progress"

🌱 Growth mindset

Gets frustrated first (that's normal), then does the real work: diagnoses exactly where she went wrong, asks a specific question, retakes and improves, and rates herself honestly with a plan to reassess later. Struggle doesn't stop her — it becomes information.

📣

Karim

Team 4 · "The Hype Man"

📣 Fake growth

Says all the right growth-mindset lines out loud, but his actions don't match: he brute-forces the test instead of learning, doesn't care about understanding a wrong answer once he's passed, rates himself "to be safe" instead of honestly, and plans to rush his project. Growth mindset language without growth mindset behaviour.

Ask teams: what did you say vs. what did the evidence show?
That gap is the real lesson.
